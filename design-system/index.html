    Overview

    Overview

    Less doom. More doing. This system covers the Crooked homepage redesign concept and everything made from it: the site, posters, socials, the newsletter header, stickers and merch. It is an unofficial design exploration, not affiliated with or endorsed by Crooked Media. Show names, show art and marks belong to their owners. Every piece carries the UnofficialMark.

    Read in this order: this page for the visual rules, Voice and tone for how it sounds, Putting it into practice for how a brief becomes a poster, a post or a product.

    The idea in one line

    Navy carries the brand, yellow does the work, and every piece ends with something to do: Listen. Show up. Organize. Keep that order whenever the three appear together.

    Color

    • Ground everything in c-navy-900 (screens use bg-inverse for bands) or paper bg-page. Paper is blue-biased c-slate-50; never pure white for the page. White (bg-surface) is for cards and boxes sitting on paper.
    • Use accent-default (yellow) for the one thing to click, the thing that's playing, focus and progress. If three things on screen are yellow, two of them are wrong.
    • Use status-live (red 500) as a fill only: live pill, live dot, strike bars, calendar strips. For red text use status-live-text (red 700 light, red 300 dark).
    • Put the player and nav on bg-chrome so tools read apart from editorial.
    • Keep con-* sky, cream and butter inside Crooked Con placements. Two sanctioned exceptions in collateral: the climate poster sky and the "Hope is a verb" sticker.
    • Pink c-pink-300 is a collateral ground (reproductive freedom, stickers) with navy type. It never appears in product UI.
    • Screen proportion: about 45 navy, 35 paper, 10 white, 7 yellow, 3 red.
    • Collateral uses the c-* primitives directly because print and social art keep fixed colors in either theme. Product UI uses semantic tokens only (bg-*, fg-*, border-*, accent-*, status-*) so it follows light and dark.

    Type

    Three faces, three jobs. Never swap them.

    • Big Shoulders Display 900 for headlines and big numbers: display-xl hero, display-l section, display-m panel, display-s sub-head, display-poster for collateral. Caps by CSS, written in sentence case so they read right in feeds and screen readers. Leading .92 on screen, .86 in collateral.
    • Public Sans for reading: lede 19px, title 16–20px bold, body 16px, body-s 14px, button 15px bold.
    • IBM Plex Mono 500 for metadata only: eyebrow 12px and label-s 11px, caps, tracking f-track-label. Show names, times, dates, counts. Tabular numerals for clocks, durations and prices.
    • Nothing a person must read goes below 11px. Body copy and buttons are never in display caps. One eyebrow per block, only when it adds a fact.

    Layout, space and shape

    • The homepage reads like a rundown: today's lineup first, the player always in reach, one band for the ways to act. Order sections by what a listener does next, not by org chart.
    • Max width layout-max (1240), gutter layout-gutter (16–48). Show grid 4 → 3 at 1000 → 2 at 720. Hero splits 1.25 : 1 and stacks at 860. Sections breathe space-16 top and bottom.
    • Spacing steps: space-1 4 through space-16 64. Card padding space-6; grid gaps space-5.
    • radius-pill for every button, chip, tag and live pill. radius-lg (12) for art and cards, radius-xl (14) for lineup rows and the player, radius-2xl (16) for panels.
    • Show art is always 1:1. Floating UI (previews, queues, checklists) gets a 2px border-strong.
    • Depth: shadow-card on hovered cards over navy, shadow-lift for popovers and the player, shadow-art for fanned art and the email calendar.

    Motion

    Move like a broadcast, not a slot machine. 150ms for hovers, 200ms for lifts and toggles, 500ms for slide changes, all on cubic-bezier(.16, .8, .24, 1). The hero dwells 8s with a visible progress bar and a pause button. Ambient motion lives in three places only (the On Crooked drift, the Con sky, the credits roll) and pauses on hover or focus. Under prefers-reduced-motion all of it stops: the drift becomes a scroll row and the credits a static list. Motion has no tokens; these values are the rule.

    Imagery, marks and icons

    • Each show's own 1:1 art, never recolored or cropped off-square. Fanned stacks rotate in 4° steps, five cards maximum. Host photography for live events, warm and candid.
    • The "CROOKED" in this system is Big Shoulders set as a stand-in (wm: 34px, 900, tracking .02em). No official logo was supplied and none is drawn here. Production must use Crooked Media's wordmark files. Vote Save America and Friends of the Pod keep their own marks; don't rebuild them in type.
    • The halo (a repeating radial ring in 13% yellow on navy) is the only decorative pattern. Collateral adds a small set of flat illustrative devices built from shapes: the sun disc, grass edge, record, calendar, checklist, scoreboard.
    • Icons: the concept uses a single inline play triangle and the live dot. There is no icon set yet; when one is needed, use simple 2px-stroke glyphs in currentColor and add them under an Icons asset group. No emoji in brand voice; emoji appear only inside quoted chat bubbles.

    Tokens

    tokens.json keeps the concept's three tiers and names. Primitives are c-*, semantic tokens are bg-*, fg-*, border-*, accent-*, status-*, focus-ring and con-*, and component tokens are button-*, play-*, chip-*. Components read semantic or component tokens, never primitives; collateral is the one exception, as above. Font families compile to --font-display, --font-body and --font-mono; bundle.css maps the concept's --f-display, --f-body and --f-mono to them so the concept's index.html keeps working.

    Fixes this system makes to the concept:

    • Red eyebrow text (3.9:1) now uses status-live-text.
    • The 9px now-playing pill is 12px bold.
    • Con type stays above the gradient midpoint.
    • Preview and queue borders use border-strong, which flips to slate in dark mode.
    • About 20 hard-coded hex values outside :root now have token names. In collateral, #9AA3C0 became c-slate-400, #33406B became c-navy-600 and #262A35 became c-chrome-800.
    • The "Early-bird ends Oct 6" line is past due; replace it with the current ticket tier or drop it.
    Voice and tone

    Voice and tone

    Talk like the smartest friend in the group chat. Crooked is for people who follow politics closely, are tired of being scared by it, and want somewhere to put the energy. The voice treats them as smart, busy and already on the team. It explains, it jokes, and it always ends with something to do.

    Four pillars

    Action over anxiety. Name the problem in one beat, then turn to the move. Every piece leaves a door open: listen, show up, or organize.

    "The news, minus the panic." · "Down, not out." · "Doomscroll less. Doorknock more."

    In on the joke. Self-aware and a little irreverent, especially about ourselves. The humor punches at the situation, never at the listener.

    "…and a few lowly podcast hosts." · "Hot takes. Yes. Twice a day, five days a week." · "Freedom & Content for All."

    Plainspoken. Short words, real verbs, no insider fog. If a sentence needs a second read, it needs a rewrite. Concrete beats clever.

    "One email each weekday evening with what happened and what you can do about it."

    Independent and proud of it. Membership keeps the network independent; it is never a donation plea. Confident, never pleading.

    "Your membership keeps Crooked independent and out of Big Tech's pocket." · "Ad-free. Fear-free."

    Writing rules

    Headlines. Two short beats split by a period, six words or fewer each. The second beat turns toward action. Write in sentence case; CSS sets the caps.

    Buttons and links. Start with a verb: Play, Join, Find, Get, See, Shop. Say what happens: "Play today's lineup", not "Learn more". A pill CTA in collateral pairs the action with the program: "Find a shift · Vote Save America".

    Body. One idea per sentence, under 25 words. Ledes stop at three sentences. Contractions always. Second person always.

    Numbers and dates. Metadata uses abbreviated months, an en dash for ranges and a middle dot as separator: Nov 5–7 · Washington, DC. Never 11/5/26. Counts in headlines are numerals: "15 days."

    Errors and UI. Literal. "That didn't load. Try again." Save the jokes for editorial.

    What the voice never does

    • Fearmonger, all-caps-shout or count down to doom.
    • Mock the listener, or anyone for being new to politics.
    • Plead for money. Membership is value first.
    • Put words in a real person's mouth. Quotes are real, attributed and checked.
    • Use emoji in brand voice. Quoted chat voices may.
    Putting it into practice

    Putting it into practice

    How a brief becomes a piece. The live examples are the Collateral components: Poster, SocialSquare, EmailBanner, StickerSheet, TeeGraphic, MerchLine.

    From brief to piece

    1. Name the action. What should the reader do after seeing this: listen to a show, show up somewhere, or organize? Pick one.
    2. Write the two beats. Beat one names the situation in the reader's words. Beat two turns it into the action. Six words or fewer each. Check it against the rewrites table.
    3. Choose a device. Most pieces need none: the headline is the art. Add one device only if it carries the idea (see Devices below).
    4. Choose the ground. Navy by default. Yellow for upbeat asks. Paper for policy claims in a box. Pink for reproductive freedom. Red for labor. Chrome for chat and app formats. Sky for climate only.
    5. Highlight one beat. On navy, the turn goes yellow. On yellow, everything is navy. Never highlight both beats.
    6. Close with the CTA and the mark. Pill CTA names the action and the program. Unofficial mark in the corner.

    Devices

    Signature moves, each tied to a piece that uses it. One per piece.

    • Strike and swap. Strike the doom word with a rotated red bar, then set the replacement in yellow. Doomscroll less. Doorknock more. · Healthcare shouldn't have a ~~paywall~~.
    • Borrowed format. Dress the ask as something familiar: the homework checklist (Democracy is a group project), the group chat (Your group chat, upgraded), the weather forecast (100% chance of turnout), the scoreboard (Home: Voters 26 · Away: Apathy 00), the album tracklist (Greatest Hits: Social Security, the 40-hour week, Medicare, Voting Rights Act, Clean Air Act; bonus track: your vote), the record sleeve (There is no planet B-side), the sequel poster (Democracy 2: The Sequel), the certificate (World's Most Persistent Phone Banker), the national-park sign (Visit your polling place). The format carries the joke; the copy carries the action.
    • This or that. Two panels, the left on white and the right on navy with the yellow answer. Hot takes / Hot planet.
    • The object. One flat object holding the date or number: the calendar (Nov 3), the ticket, the checklist box.
    • The scene. Flat shapes only: sun disc with two soft rings, a navy grass edge, mountains for the park badge. No photos, no people.
    • The seal. Dashed yellow ring around a navy ring, mono line on top, display lockup inside. Est. somewhere between doom and doing.

    Merch rules

    • One line per product, drawn from the house lines.
    • Ink from the brand palette; garment colors may extend it (black, forest, natural canvas, duck tan) and stay off screens.
    • Series share one idea. Everybody In: Everybody in · Raise the floor · Everybody eats · Working people first · All of us · Build it here.
    • Caption mockups as PRODUCT NAME over a mono spec line: "Planet B-side bucket hat · Navy twill · yellow embroidery".

    Before it ships

    • [ ] Two beats, six words or fewer each, second beat is the action.
    • [ ] One yellow highlight, one device, one CTA.
    • [ ] Type above the sky midpoint; no butter or cream on c-sky-300.
    • [ ] Red only as fill, or at display size; red text is c-red-700.
    • [ ] Dates as Nov 3 / Nov 5–7 · Washington, DC; nothing past due.
    • [ ] The unofficial mark is present and legible.
    • [ ] No real person quoted or pictured without a source; no official marks rebuilt in type.
    Midterm messaging

    Midterm messaging

    A campaign layer on top of the voice for the final stretch before the Nov 3, 2026 midterms. Same voice (action over anxiety, in on the joke), aimed at four targets. The live set is the MidtermSet component. These lines are dated: retire or rewrite them after Nov 3.

    The four lanes

    Every piece picks one lane, lands one joke, and ends with one action.

    1. The cost of everything

    The everyday bill: gas, groceries, rent, child care, tariffs. The joke is the gap between "prices are coming down" and the receipt.

    • Gas is $4.20. Not a joke. (Neither is Tuesday.)
    • Read the receipt. Then read the ballot.
    • They called affordability a con job. Your receipt calls it Tuesday.
    • "The party of affordability." Have you met a receipt?
    • Your cart got smaller. Your ballot didn't.
    • Fill the tank. Then fill in the bubble.
    • Tax cuts at the top. Price hikes at the pump.

    2. Check, please (the gold-plated White House)

    The gold decor, the ballroom, the paved Rose Garden, and the Cheesecake Factory comparison that took over the week. The joke is the menu: lavish for them, nothing on it for you. Close the tab is the action.

    • Check, please. (Close the tab · Vote Nov 3)
    • Less Cheesecake Factory. More factories.
    • Donors got a ballroom. You got the bill.
    • Gold everything. Nothing on the menu for you.
    • Affordability: 86'd.
    • Your table's ready. Polls close at the end of the night.

    3. For sale (the grift)

    Self-dealing, donor favors, family business in the people's house. The joke is the swamp that got remodeled instead of drained.

    • Drain the swamp? They put in a hot tub.
    • Everything's for sale but your vote.
    • Not a cabinet. A gift shop.
    • The swamp got a renovation. You got the invoice.

    4. Make it a short term (turnout)

    The close. Every piece in the other three lanes can end here.

    • Midterms Tuesday. Make it a short term.
    • Four days. Every vote.
    • Costume Saturday. Ballot Tuesday.
    • The polls close. We don't.
    • Democracy is a group project. Your part is due Nov 3.

    Round 2 lines

    Built into MidtermSet (round 2 row) and rotating in HeadlineReel.

    Cost of everything - Groceries cost a fortune. Voting's still free. - The price is wrong. (Price tag: "Coming down" struck, "Still up".) - Inflation isn't a vibe. It's a receipt. - Eggs are a luxury good now. Vote like it. - Tariffs: a tax with better PR. - "Prices are coming down," they said. So is our patience.

    Check, please - Huge portions. Tiny priorities. - The menu's 20 pages long. None of them say "child care." - All that gold and still no plan for groceries. - Gilded Age? We've seen the sequel.

    For sale - The art of the steal. (Pair it with a sourced example, like the donor-funded ballroom.) - The only thing trickling down is the bill. - Draining the swamp, one family business at a time. - Self-dealing isn't a jobs program. - Make America Grift Again. (Parodies a trademarked slogan: editorial and social only, never merch.)

    Working people - Billionaires don't clip coupons. (You do. Vote like it.) - Work hard. Pay more. That's not the deal. - The middle class isn't a talking point. It's your Tuesday.

    Make it a short term - Don't let the midterms be mid. - Midterms: no extensions. - Doomscrolling isn't voting. - Pick a polling place, not a fight. - Your vote is the one receipt they can't lose.

    Rules for this round

    • Facts first, then the joke. Every number is sourced and dated in the piece's notes: the $4.20 gas figure is the AAA national average for Oct 30, 2026. When a number moves, the line moves with it.
    • Quote only what was said. "The party of affordability" and "con job" are the president's own words about affordability. Attribute or set them as quotes; never invent a quote or put words in a named person's mouth.
    • Punch at conduct, not people. Mock the gold, the ballroom, the receipts, the self-dealing. No name-calling, no jokes about anyone's body, family or voters.
    • Grift, not crimes. Say grift, self-dealing, for sale, donor's choice. Don't call anything a crime unless a charge or conviction says so.
    • The Cheesecake Factory is the punchline, not the target. The chain and its diners are fine. The joke is the decor in the White House. No logos, no menu art, no trade dress.
    • Merch at the polls. Some states restrict political clothing at polling places. Never promote merch as something to wear to vote.
    • Every piece ends with an action: Vote Nov 3, make a plan, bring three friends, find a shift. Polling hours and rules vary by state; never print a single nationwide opening or closing time.
    • Grounds follow the system: navy for cost of living, yellow for Check, please and turnout, red for who pays, chrome for grift, paper for receipts and other borrowed formats.

    Components

    Components and collateral

    Actions

    Button

    Primary, ghost, ink

    Guidelines

    Pill buttons whose label is a verb phrase that says what happens.

    Provide: an <a> or <button> with ck-btn plus one variant class, and a label that starts with a verb (Play, Join, Find, Get, See, Shop).

    Variant Class Use
    Primaryck-btn-primaryYellow (button-primary-bg) with navy label (button-primary-fg, 11.8:1). One per view, on navy or paper.
    Ghostck-btn-ghostSecondary action beside a primary. Inherits the text color of its band.
    Inkck-btn-inkNavy with a yellow label. Use on yellow and sky grounds where a yellow button would vanish.
    • Do write "Play today's lineup", "Find a shift", "Get tickets".
    • Don't write "Learn more", "Click here", or set the label in display caps.
    • Don't put two primary buttons in one view. If three things are yellow, two are wrong.
    • Radius is always radius-pill; padding is button-padding; label style is button.
    Actions

    Chip

    Filter chips

    Guidelines

    Pill filters for the show grid.

    Provide: <button class="ck-chip"> with aria-pressed set; the selected state is driven by aria-pressed="true".

    • Filter only. Never use a chip as a link or a tag that does nothing.
    • Selected: chip-selected-bg with chip-selected-fg (navy and yellow in light; slate and navy in dark).
    • Labels are short nouns in sentence case: "Daily news", "Law & courts".
    Actions

    PlayButton

    44 and compact 38

    Guidelines

    The yellow disc that starts audio.

    Provide: a <button class="ck-play"> with an aria-label naming the episode, and a visible title next to it. Add compact inside the player bar.

    • Size play-size (44px, also the minimum touch target); play-size-compact (38px) only inside the player, where the whole row is the target.
    • Fill play-bg, glyph play-fg.
    • Never a play button without a title beside it; never a second yellow action competing in the same row.
    Status

    Eyebrow

    Mono metadata

    Guidelines

    Mono caps metadata above a headline.

    Provide: <span class="ck-eyebrow"> with facts only: show names, durations, dates, counts.

    • Style eyebrow: IBM Plex Mono 500, 12px, tracking f-track-label, color fg-muted (or fg-on-inverse-muted on navy).
    • Separate facts with a middle dot; ranges take an en dash: Nov 5–7 · Washington, DC.
    • One eyebrow per block and only when it adds a fact. Never on every card by habit.

    LivePill

    Live and now playing

    Guidelines
