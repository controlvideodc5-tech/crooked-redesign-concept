# Putting it into practice

How a brief becomes a piece. The live examples are the **Collateral** components: `Poster`, `SocialSquare`, `EmailBanner`, `StickerSheet`, `TeeGraphic`, `MerchLine`.

## From brief to piece

1. **Name the action.** What should the reader do after seeing this: listen to a show, show up somewhere, or organize? Pick one.
2. **Write the two beats.** Beat one names the situation in the reader's words. Beat two turns it into the action. Six words or fewer each. Check it against the rewrites table.
3. **Choose a device.** Most pieces need none: the headline is the art. Add one device only if it carries the idea (see Devices below).
4. **Choose the ground.** Navy by default. Yellow for upbeat asks. Paper for policy claims in a box. Pink for reproductive freedom. Red for labor. Chrome for chat and app formats. Sky for climate only.
5. **Highlight one beat.** On navy, the turn goes yellow. On yellow, everything is navy. Never highlight both beats.
6. **Close with the CTA and the mark.** Pill CTA names the action and the program. Unofficial mark in the corner.

## Devices

Signature moves, each tied to a piece that uses it. One per piece.

- **Strike and swap.** Strike the doom word with a rotated red bar, then set the replacement in yellow. *Doomscroll less. Doorknock more.* · *Healthcare shouldn't have a ~~paywall~~.*
- **Borrowed format.** Dress the ask as something familiar: the homework checklist (*Democracy is a group project*), the group chat (*Your group chat, upgraded*), the weather forecast (*100% chance of turnout*), the scoreboard (*Home: Voters 26 · Away: Apathy 00*), the album tracklist (*Greatest Hits*: Social Security, the 40-hour week, Medicare, Voting Rights Act, Clean Air Act; bonus track: your vote), the record sleeve (*There is no planet B-side*), the sequel poster (*Democracy 2: The Sequel*), the certificate (*World's Most Persistent Phone Banker*), the national-park sign (*Visit your polling place*). The format carries the joke; the copy carries the action.
- **The fake small business.** Name a deadpan contractor after a public project and let the trade tagline do the rest: *Big Balls Demolition & Remodel*, *Hard Letters Signage Co.*, *Lincoln Blue Pool & Paint*, *Triumphal Erection Co.* Set it like real workwear (chest logo, back print, sincere tagline) and show it next to the landmark it points at.
- **This or that.** Two panels, the left on white and the right on navy with the yellow answer. *Hot takes / Hot planet.*
- **The object.** One flat object holding the date or number: the calendar (*Nov 3*), the ticket, the checklist box.
- **The scene.** Flat shapes only: sun disc with two soft rings, a navy grass edge, mountains for the park badge. No photos, no people.
- **The seal.** Dashed yellow ring around a navy ring, mono line on top, display lockup inside. *Est. somewhere between doom and doing.*

## Merch rules

- One line per product, drawn from the house lines.
- Ink from the brand palette; garment colors may extend it (black, forest, natural canvas, duck tan) and stay off screens.
- Series share one idea. *Everybody In*: Everybody in · Raise the floor · Everybody eats · Working people first · All of us · Build it here.
- Satire aims at projects and policies, never at a named person's face or name. Every factual premise is true and dated.
- Caption mockups as PRODUCT NAME over a mono spec line: "Planet B-side bucket hat · Navy twill · yellow embroidery".

## Before it ships

- [ ] Two beats, six words or fewer each, second beat is the action.
- [ ] One yellow highlight, one device, one CTA.
- [ ] Type above the sky midpoint; no butter or cream on `c-sky-300`.
- [ ] Red only as fill, or at display size; red text is `c-red-700`.
- [ ] Dates as `Nov 3` / `Nov 5–7 · Washington, DC`; nothing past due.
- [ ] The unofficial mark is present and legible.
- [ ] No real person quoted or pictured without a source; no official marks rebuilt in type.
